The loop

Every frame: apply gravity to velocity, velocity to position, then check collisions. That's the whole engine — three lines and a handful of ifs.

Which way a brick bounces

On contact the ball overlaps the brick on both axes. Reflecting the axis with the smaller overlap is what makes edge hits and corner hits behave the way your eye expects.

Substepping

A fast ball can cross the paddle between two frames and miss it entirely. The movement is split into small slices so that can't happen.

Splitting

A large rock breaks into two mediums, a medium into two smalls. Small rocks pay 25, large ones only 5 — the points are in the follow-up, not the first shot.

Letting them through

Nothing happens when a rock reaches the floor. Ignoring a large one is often safer than turning it into four fast-moving fragments.

Banking lives

Get the sky back round to black — one full colour cycle — and you earn a life. The backdrop is the clock: watch for orange and you know one is close.

The gunship

From the first grey wave it crosses near the top, either way, firing rounds that pass straight through rock. Worth 150 — more than anything else on screen.

Spinners

The red diamonds end the run if they touch the floor — lives don't save you. But one drifting off the side is harmless, so read the angle before you spend shots on it.

Why you cannot reverse

A turn straight back into your own neck is rejected rather than fatal — otherwise a mistimed swipe would end the run with no way to react.

The tail is not in the way

The cell your tail is leaving this step is free to move into, so following your own tail round is safe. Only growing changes that.

Speed is length

Every segment shortens the tick a little, so the difficulty curve is something you build yourself by playing well.

Tap, or swipe

A tap hops forward; a tap to the left or right of the frog hops that way. Swipes work in every direction, back included. Each new row you reach pays 10.

The river is the hard half

Bays and flies

Burn toward your finger

Holding anywhere fires the engine, and the craft leans toward wherever your finger is. Let go and it rights itself while it falls.

What counts as a landing

Both feet on a pad, falling slower than the legs can take, close to upright and barely drifting. The telemetry turns green when you are inside every limit.

Fuel is the clock

Pads pay 50 times their multiplier, doubled for a perfect touchdown. A landing refunds fuel; a crash costs 150. The run ends when the tank is dry.

Your thumb stays out of the way

Drag anywhere and the ship moves as far as your finger does, so you never cover it. Holding down fires. You can fly forward and back as well as up and down.

Anything you touch kills you

Rock, an enemy or one of their shots each cost a ship. A new ship blinks for two seconds, safe from enemies — but not from the walls.

Squadrons pay extra

Darts 100, weavers 150, divers 200, gunners 250 (two hits). Down a whole squadron for 300 more, and clear a wave for 250 times its number.
